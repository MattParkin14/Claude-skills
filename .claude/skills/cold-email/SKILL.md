# Cold Email Playbook

Distilled from 11 YouTube videos by practitioners who collectively sent 10M+ cold emails. Ranked by how many sources independently endorsed each action.

---

## 1. Personalise with something only *they* would recognise — **11/11 sources**

This is the non-negotiable. The bar has risen: first name + company name is table stakes. You need something specific to this human.

**What to personalise on:**
- A LinkedIn post they wrote about their dog, a recent business milestone, a passion mentioned in their bio
- A pain point you found in their job listing or open roles
- A trigger event: they just joined the company, their company was just funded, they just published a case study
- A shared connection, fraternity, school, city, or interest

**Subject line version (most powerful):** Make it so specific it makes no sense to anyone else.
> "Switzerland + liip cheeseburger + [Your Company]" — sent to a Swiss executive who waxes poetic about a specific DC burger joint. She replied within 10 hours.

You can reuse the *same personalised subject pattern* across executives who share that trait (all in the same fraternity, all Swiss, etc.) — scales while feeling 1:1.

**First sentence:** Write it as if messaging a new acquaintance you just met at an event. Prove you looked at them specifically.

**Why it works:** Recipients feel the need to *reciprocate* effort. Generic blasts fail because there's nothing to reciprocate.

## 2. Keep it short enough to read on an iPhone without scrolling — **9/11 sources**

Every successful template converged on the same constraint: one sentence per section, total email fits on a phone screen.

**The 3-line structure (most minimal):**
1. Personalised poke-the-bear question about their situation
2. Simple value prop (what you do + who you've done it for)
3. Low-pressure CTA

**The SCCS framework (fuller version):**
- **S** — Subject line
- **C** — Custom compliment (1 sentence: "Huge fan of [Company], love the work you did on [specific thing]")
- **C** — Case study (1 sentence: "Recently I generated 400 leads in 30 days for a roofing company")
- **S** — Signature (Name, Title, Address, Phone, LinkedIn)

Hard rule: one sentence per section. If you can't read it without scrolling on an iPhone, cut it.

## 3. Lead with their pain, not your credentials — **9/11 sources**

**Don't:**
> "We provide 10 years of commercial cleaning experience. We do X, Y, and Z. I'd love to send you a quote."

**Do:**
> "How do you ensure your operatories and lobby stay consistently clean and compliant for patients and families?"
> "Hey Andrew, noticed Workday still hasn't filled their digital sales manager role. Bummer."

**Value proposition = the challenge you solve, not what you do.** Name their problem. Then tie your offer to that specific problem.

**Echo marketing:** Transcribe your sales calls. Pull the exact sentences buyers use to describe their pain in private — the specific, visceral language ("we were two months away from shutting our doors," "I pay payroll and finish the month with $0 of profit"). Use that verbatim in your emails. Readers feel you're reading their mind because you're echoing back words someone exactly like them already said.

## 4. Use proof that matches their niche — **8/11 sources**

Generic case studies don't move people. Matched case studies do.

- "Recently I generated 400 leads in 30 days for a **roofing company**" → sent to a roofing company (not "a service business")
- Name-drop clients they'd *recognise or relate to* — companies in their space, at their size
- Find their own case studies on their website and reference them: "I saw how you helped Intercom do XYZ — impressive" signals research and can be automated via Clay/Centiment

If you're new and have no case studies: use a "sniper email" approach — offer a specific, almost-free deliverable ("I have a template that helps [outcome]. Mind if I send it over?") and only create it once they say yes. The implied effort builds credibility.

## 5. CTA: low-commitment, open-ended, you control the next step — **8/11 sources**

**Don't:**
- Send a Calendly link — presumptuous, puts the scheduling burden on them
- Name a specific time ("Monday at 1pm") — lets them say no to the slot instead of yes to meeting

**Do:**
- "Mind if I send over a few times for a call?"
- "Would you be open to a complimentary estimate over the next week?"
- "Do you have time over the next week or two to learn more? Let me know what works and I'll send a calendar invite."
- "Want me to send more info?"
- "Mind if I send it over?" (sniper email version — lowest friction possible)

**Disagreement — specific time vs open-ended:**
V11 (Dan Martell) recommends naming a specific short window: "Do you have 7 minutes tomorrow at 2pm?" His reasoning: specificity reduces decision fatigue. V5 (Sam McKenna, 43% open rate) says this lets them say no to the slot. **Judgment call:** specific times work better for warm/referral leads; open-ended works better for cold strangers.

## 6. Three email sequences max, then recycle — **6/11 sources**

From 10M+ emails: email 1 always performs best. Emails 4+ mostly annoy and get you marked spam.

**Sequence structure:**
- **Email 1:** Why you + why now (trigger event) → clear offer in one sentence → social proof (niche case study) → soft CTA
- **Email 2 (3–5 days later):** Add context you *cut* from email 1. Thread it as a reply so they can see the original. Don't repeat; expand.
- **Email 3 (3–5 days later):** Lower the friction. Don't remind them they ignored you. Change the CTA: offer a Loom audit, a lead magnet, or ask a simpler yes/no question. Use a **fresh subject line** (not a thread reply) so they don't see the ignored chain.

**Recycle your list every 60–90 days** with a fresh angle. They won't remember your email. Their business priorities change. Timing is everything in cold outreach.

**Disagreement on sequence length:** V11 and V3 advocate for 4+ follow-ups. V9 (10M emails data) shows hard diminishing returns after 3. Compromise: run 3 emails, recycle with a completely new campaign rather than extending the same sequence.

## 7. Infrastructure: multiple warmed accounts, low volume per inbox — **5/11 sources**

- Max **30 emails/day/inbox**
- Use secondary domains (tryYourBrand.com, getYourBrand.com) — up to 3 accounts per domain
- Warm each domain for **3 weeks** before sending
- Tools: Instantly.ai or Smartlead handle this automatically
- Target **40–60% open rate** as a deliverability health signal. Below 30% = deliverability problem.
- When deliverability fails: don't bother with seed tests or Glock Apps. Just set up new domains, rewrite your copy, restart.

## 8. Use trigger events to make timing your edge — **5/11 sources**

Best triggers by performance (from 10M+ emails):

| Trigger | Signal strength |
|---|---|
| Person recently posted on LinkedIn about a topic you can tie to your offer | Best of 2024 |
| Person recently joined a new company | Best of 2023 |
| Company founded in last 2 years | High |
| Company recently funded | High |
| CEO is first-time CEO | High (unique messaging angle) |
| Company has an open role your offer makes irrelevant | High |
| Their recent case study mentioning a client like yours | Medium (shows research) |

Use Clay to waterfall these — stack triggers, change messaging based on which combo is true.

**New role opener example:**
> "Hey [Name], congrats on the new role as [Title]. As you're taking over, I'm guessing you're looking to shake things up..."

## 9. Subject line and preview text tactics — **7/11 sources**

**Subject lines that work:**
- Deeply personal and specific (makes no sense to anyone else) — highest reply rate
- "Quick question" — simple, still works
- All lowercase — feels like a text from a friend, not a promo email
- Start with "re:" — looks like a thread, drives opens out of curiosity

**Preview text** (the snippet after the subject in the inbox view):
- 24% of people decide to open based on preview text alone
- Don't let it default to "Hey John blah blah..." — set it manually to your most curiosity-inducing hook
- Treat it as a second subject line

---

## 10. Give value before you ask — **6/11 sources**

**PPC formula (most effective copywriting structure):**
> Pain point → Partial solution (enough to intrigue, not enough to solve) → CTA to get the full solution

Example:
> "Hey Scott, noticed your team is spending a ton on Clutch sponsorships. Did you know that tweaking your profile wording daily can rank you higher organically? Clients I've done this for kept their lead flow after cancelling sponsorships, saving them thousands. If you like that, I have 3 more ideas. Let's do a call this week."

**Loom audit variant:** Email references a specific problem → attach a free personalised Loom audit → CTA *inside the Loom* is to book a call for the full fix. Combines value delivery with an open loop.

**Ghost product / sacrificial lamb:** Voluntarily acknowledge something they can get elsewhere cheaper or that doesn't apply to them. "You can get X cheaper at Costco — don't buy that from us." After that one concession, everything you *do* recommend is trusted. Close rate climbs to near 100% in Hormozi's retail test.

---

## 11. Have an online presence — **3/11 sources**

Prospects Google you before replying. If you're a ghost — no website, no LinkedIn activity — they don't respond. Have a basic website and post on social occasionally. This is a silent prerequisite.

## Workflow: putting it together

### Before you write a single email

1. **Narrow your ICP first.** A 20-person company's Director of Marketing is a completely different person from a 500-person company's. Use Clay or Apollo filters to cut the list to people your offer actually fits. Bad targeting kills good copy.
2. **Identify your best trigger.** Look at your current best customers — what did they have in common? Use that as your primary filter.
3. **Pull buyer language.** From sales calls, from testimonials, from reviews. Use their words, not yours.

### Writing the email

1. **Subject line:** Personalised-specific OR "quick question" / lowercase / "re:" depending on volume/context
2. **Line 1:** Personalised hook proving you looked at *them*
3. **Line 2:** Their pain or a trigger-based observation (not your credentials)
4. **Line 3:** One-sentence social proof / case study matched to their niche
5. **Line 4:** Low-commitment, open-ended CTA
6. **Signature:** Name, Title, Address (compliance), Phone. Skip the logo and image — plain text lands in primary inbox.
7. **Preview text:** Set manually to your best hook
8. **PS line:** Second-most-read element after subject. Use for a CTA, a stat, or a meme that reinforces the message.

### Avoid these words and patterns
- "Free" → use "complimentary"
- Calendar links in first email
- More than 2 links total (signals spam)
- Money language in subject ("earn," "revenue," "profit") → promo tab
- Images in B2B cold email → promo tab
- Attachments → spam folder

### Follow-up sequence
| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Email 1 (full pitch) |
| 3–5 | Email 2 (add context, thread reply) |
| 8–10 | Email 3 (lower friction, fresh subject line) |
| +60 days | Recycle with completely new angle |

### Small TAM (<20,000 leads)
Don't thread individual touches (email → call → LinkedIn to the same person in sequence). Instead: email *everyone* → cold call *everyone* → LinkedIn message *everyone* → direct mail *everyone*. Different people respond to different channels; running each channel to completion outperforms elaborate per-person sequencing.
