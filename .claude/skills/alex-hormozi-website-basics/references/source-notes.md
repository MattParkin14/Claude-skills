# Source notes — Alex Hormozi website basics

Single source: a YouTube breakdown in which an agency operator (800+ websites
built, startups through Fortune 500) plays clips of Alex Hormozi on conversion
and translates each one into web design. Distilled 2026-08-11.

Verbatim quotes below — use them when the user wants the original framing, or
when attribution matters. Everything else lives in `SKILL.md`.

## Why conversion is the leverage

> "Neil Patel said in one of his keynote speeches that he believes the future of
> marketing is going to exist in CRO. And he said that because the prices are
> going to only consistently go up, and most platforms are based on an auction,
> which means multiple businesses competing for the same eyeballs. And so the
> people who are better able to convert those eyeballs are going to be the ones
> who outcompete and win." — Hormozi

Operator's gloss: "It's the single most expensive thing business owners get
wrong. Everybody obsesses over traffic — more ads, more reach. Hardly anyone
obsesses over what happens when that traffic lands... they're burning cash on
traffic that was always going to bounce."

## Fewest variables

> "The people who make the best decisions focus on the fewest variables that make
> the biggest difference." — Hormozi

Gloss: "Most people redesign the whole thing and change nothing that actually
mattered. Do less, but do it on the things that count."

## One split test a week

> "The process is simple. Every week what we do is we have our marketing meeting
> and we do one split test. Just one. And that's because everyone wants to test
> 100 things. But what it does is it forces us to be very, very focused on the
> one thing that we think is going to drive the highest result." — Hormozi

Gloss: "Most business owners redesign the whole website every year and they learn
nothing, because they changed 40 things at once and can't tell which one worked."

## The two tests that matter

> "I'll give you the two biggest things that we look at. The first thing — it
> sounds obvious and simple, but people don't do it — we simply split test the
> headlines on our landing pages. That's it. The next thing we'll split test is
> going to be the image that is underneath of the headline. Because what we've
> seen from the vast majority of all the tests we've done is that almost all of
> the juice, the biggest jumps in conversion rate, happen above the fold."
> — Hormozi

Gloss on why: above the fold is "the only part of your site you can be certain
that every visitor sees", the judgment takes "about half a second", and it
"colors everything else — if it looks cheap up top, they'll read your best
testimonials with doubt."

On imagery: "Everyone can spot a stock photo... the moment they clock it, a
little voice asks, what else on this site is fake? With AI flooding the internet
with perfect, soulless design right now, something that looks like a real human
touched it has become the strongest trust signal you've got."

## The Value Equation

> "Number one is the dream outcome — is the thing that we are promising something
> that people actually want? Number two is the perceived likelihood of
> achievement — how likely does someone believe that if they purchase your thing,
> they're going to achieve the outcome we are promising? Number three is the time
> component — how quickly are they going to get the thing after they pay? The
> fourth is the dual-sided coin of effort and sacrifice." — Hormozi

Graphic supplied with the source:

```
              DREAM OUTCOME  ×  PERCEIVED LIKELIHOOD OF ACHIEVEMENT
   VALUE  =  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                  TIME DELAY  ×  EFFORT & SACRIFICE
```

## Where the fortunes are

> "Most people, myself included, spent the first half to two-thirds of my career
> trying to beef up the top side. I would promise bigger, and I would show more
> testimonials, more results. But I believe now — and I've done this a little bit
> — that the actual fortunes rely on the bottom side of the equation." — Hormozi

## Speed

> "Speed is one of the best all-time competitive advantages." — Hormozi

Gloss: "People hear speed and think about their product and their delivery. On a
website, speed starts before any of that. It's how fast your page loads and how
fast someone can understand what you do."

## Selling against inaction

> "Most people think that they're selling against their competition. I disagree.
> What you're really selling against is inaction. You're selling against doubt.
> You're selling against the biggest obstacle, which is risk. The comfort of not
> doing the thing or not buying the product is so high, we have to help decrease
> their action threshold. We have to lower that bar just so they can step right
> over it... so there's so little friction for them to take action with us that
> they buy." — Hormozi

## Amazon and perceived likelihood

> "If you look at Amazon, they sell the same products as everyone else,
> literally. But what did they do? They increased the perceived likelihood of
> achievement by adding all the reviews. So people believe that when they buy the
> thing, they're going to get the outcome they really want." — Hormozi

Gloss on guarantees: "Make it a specific guarantee. The vague 'satisfaction
guaranteed' badge is the kind everyone's brain skips straight past. Spell out
exactly what happens if it doesn't work." And on placement: "I put these right
around the call to action button on purpose, because that's the exact spot where
the doubt spikes — that half second before someone actually commits."

## The operator's own addition (not Hormozi)

> "If your website looks like every other business in your industry, you'll get
> priced like every other business in your industry. All the conversion tactics in
> the world still leave you as a commodity if the whole thing could belong to any
> of your competitors, and commodities will forever compete on price... In a
> market full of identical AI-generated pages, the ones that win still read like a
> real business with a real opinion."

## Closing summary

> "The whole game is conversion. You win it above the fold, you sell the result
> instead of the website, and you kill the risk before they click."
