# cold-sms

Turn cold phone numbers into booked calls with text messaging. This playbook distills 17 practitioner videos into one workflow: **write openers that get a reply → move fast to a call → follow up relentlessly → keep your numbers healthy and compliant.**

The single governing idea across every source: **treat SMS as a one-to-one conversation, not a broadcast.** The first text's only job is to earn a reply. Everything else builds on that.

## When to use this

Writing or reviewing cold/warm text outreach, designing a follow-up cadence, building reply/objection scripts, setting up sending infrastructure, or diagnosing why texts aren't converting.

## Core principles (apply to everything below)

1. **Conversation, not campaign.** The opener opens a thread. It does not pitch, sell, or link.
2. **Feel safe to reply to.** In the first ~3 seconds the recipient asks "is this a scam?" Disarm: identify yourself, be specific and human, ask an easy question.
3. **Speed-to-lead wins.** Getting a reply is the easy part; responding within minutes is the skill.
4. **The money is in the follow-up.** Most bookings come after 5+ touches, not the first message.
5. **Consistency beats intensity.** A few hundred texts every weekday, dripped, beats a monthly blast — for both results and number health.
6. **Comply on purpose.** Register (A2P/10DLC), identify yourself, honor opt-outs, don't text landlines. Write every message as if a compliance reviewer will read it.

## Step 1 — Qualify the fit (niche + data)

The system lives or dies on niche and data quality, not copy. Best-fit niches have: owner mobile numbers publicly available, urgent/high-ticket needs, a reply culture (owner-operators live on their phones), a business (not consumer) recipient, and a single "yes" that can move the deal. Trades, home services, real estate, staffing, and local B2B score high.

**Data hygiene is mandatory before sending:**
- Source names + mobile numbers + city (e.g. Apollo, D7 Lead Finder, or scraping tools).
- **Remove landlines** (you can't text them; trying tanks deliverability and risks suspension) — verify line type first.
- Dedupe, drop bad/old numbers, format phones with country code and no spaces/dashes (`+1XXXXXXXXXX`).
- Segment by niche/location. Keep a **2-week backlog** of leads so you never run dry.

## Step 2 — Write the opener (the "two-liner")

The most important message. Maximum two short lines. Its only goal: a positive reply.

**Structure:**
- **Line 1** — who you are + why you're texting, in a human tone: `Hey {name}, this is {you} from {company}.`
- **Line 2** — one specific, believable reason + a low-friction closed (yes/no) question tied to *their* situation.

**The two-text opener (recommended).** Send Line 1 alone, then Line 2 about 5 seconds later — the gap makes it feel like a person typing, not a system.

**Rules:**
- **No links in the first text. Ever.** Book by phone/text instead.
- Small numbers and real place names feel real; big numbers and vague claims feel fake.
- Frame yourself as bringing them value, not selling. Avoid the words "leads," "marketing," "consulting," "appointments."
- Keep the ask tiny ("Want me to send details?" / "Are you still taking on X?"), not "when can we schedule a call?" — that's too big for a stranger.
- Have a "where'd you get my number?" answer ready ("found it on Google Maps") — it diffuses suspicion instantly.

**Examples (adapt — never send verbatim; identical templates die):**
- `Hey John, are you open to taking on more roofing jobs in Dayton?`
- `Hey Steve, it's Alex from Northflow. Are you still taking on bathroom remodels, or did you stop?`
- `Hi Sarah, this is Chris — are you investing money right now?` (financial coaching)

**Two frames — test both, decide on signed clients not replies:**
- **Free-value-first:** offer a small slice of result up front ("I can send you two of these free — if they're solid we can talk"). Use when the market is burned or you can deliver a quick win.
- **Direct:** name a specific paid outcome with a small commitment. Use when your proof is strong.

## Step 3 — Reply fast and move to a call

When a positive reply lands:
1. **Match their tone** — calm, human, not robotic or over-eager. ("Awesome, glad to hear it.")
2. **Confirm context** if needed (who you are / what you mentioned).
3. **Offer a small next step** — low friction. ("Free tomorrow afternoon for a quick call?")
4. **Then CALL within a minute** of that text. This dramatically raises the book rate.

**The text's job is to get the call — not to sell.** Never quote price, negotiate, or send the offer over text. If they push "what's your offer?", redirect to a call or a property/needs question.

**The call (≤5 min, casual not corporate):** Open (reestablish context) → Intent ("just seeing if what we do even fits") → Qualify (2–3 short niche questions that surface a little pain) → Value bridge (tie their answer to your result) → **Book as soon as possible** (today > tomorrow morning > tomorrow afternoon; the sooner, the higher the show rate — never 3–4 days out without reminders).

## Step 4 — Follow up relentlessly (this is where the money is)

Most deals come *after* the first message. Roughly 80% of bookings land after 5+ follow-ups; sources cite the 5th–12th touch as the sweet spot.

**Cold (no reply to the opener):** follow up ~2 more times, then stop. A non-reply is usually not a hard no (busy / didn't trust the number / offer unclear / waiting to see if you follow up). Timing: follow-up 1 at ~2–3h, follow-up 2 at ~24h — offset by an hour or two so timestamps never look automated. Keep them vague and curious ("Wanted to make sure my last message didn't get lost — you still taking on jobs right now?").

**Warm (replied but not booked):** this is the goal state. Aim for ~16 touches over 2 weeks (≈8 texts + 8 calls, every other day). The objective is **phone-number familiarity** — once they recognize and trust the number, they book. Each text is one blunt line ending in an easy choice ("does today or tomorrow work better?"). Never restart the pitch. Leave short voicemails on unanswered calls.

**Make replying effortless.** For stalled/warm leads, a multiple-choice "slot close" works well:
> Haven't heard back — it's one of these, just pick a letter: A) been busy B) went another direction C) changed your mind D) none of the above.

**Mix media** on follow-ups — voice notes, a short selfie video, a meme, or an iMessage tap-back — instead of repeating the same words. Different formats get different reactions and are harder to ignore.

**Handle objections: Validate → Relate → Redirect.** An objection is hesitation, not rejection — they lack trust, clarity, timing, need, or feel cost/risk. Acknowledge first ("Totally fair"), relate ("most of my best clients said the same"), then redirect to a call. Handle once, then move on; never argue.

**Reactivate the database.** Non-responders after the sequence → wait 2–3 weeks → monthly reactivation ("Following up from last month — still open to chat?"). This converts well because the prior thread is visible and the number is now familiar.

## Step 5 — Sending infrastructure & deliverability

Good copy dies on bad infrastructure. A CRM built for this (e.g. GoHighLevel with Lead Connector, or similar) handles most of it.

- **Register A2P / 10DLC before sending.** Unregistered = throttled/blocked. Needs a registered business, matching domain + email, privacy/terms pages, and sample messages. Approval takes a couple of days. Tip: follow the carrier/platform approval guide's wording closely, then adapt to your industry.
- **Use a local-area-code number** matching your target region — a familiar area code reads as safe. Texting a region from a foreign number reads as a scam.
- **Include compliant opt-out language** and always identify yourself.
- **Drip, never blast.** Send ~1 message every few minutes, not a whole list at once — blasting flags you as spam and burns numbers/accounts. Restrict to daytime, weekdays.
- **Run multiple numbers, rotate them, warm new ones (7–10 days low volume), and cap ~50–150 sends/day/number.** More numbers = better deliverability. Pause a number that starts getting cautioned.
- **Rotate 5–10 message variations** (each slightly different) to protect deliverability and to split-test.
- **Auto-stop on errors:** when a number errors/bounces (invalid/landline), set it to do-not-disturb so you stop paying to text a dead number and stop hurting your reputation. Keep error rate low (aim under ~3%).
- **iMessage vs SMS:** blue-bubble iMessage gets markedly higher reply rates than green SMS and feels more trustworthy — great for warm/verified sending. Caution: cold-blasting from a personal Apple ID/iMessage can get the account banned, so treat high-volume cold sending and iMessage as separate strategies.

## Step 6 — Track and optimize

Measure daily: **messages sent, replies, positive replies, appointments booked.** Derive reply rate, positive-reply rate, booking rate, and positive-reply→booked rate.

**Rough benchmarks (plan conservatively):**
- Appointment booking rate: plan at **1%** (2% is good, 5% is elite). At 1%, ~2,000–2,500 sends/month ≈ one booked call per weekday.
- Positive-reply → booked: aim **~40%** over the 2-week follow-up.
- Delivery rate > 90%; reply rate > 10%.

**Optimize scientifically:** change one variable at a time, keep what improves booked-calls-per-100-sends, and test each change 30–50 times before judging. Decide on *signed clients*, not vanity replies. Expect a ~2-week latency before the pipeline reflects your follow-up.

**Common failure modes:** blasting instead of dripping · texting landlines · unregistered numbers · no speed-to-lead · quitting after one follow-up · no tracking · ignoring opt-outs · inconsistency.

## Compliance guardrails (do not skip)

- Register (A2P/10DLC), identify yourself in every message, and honor opt-outs / STOP immediately.
- Keep a light audit trail (number source, import date, message dates, opt-outs) so you can answer "where did you get my number?".
- Don't text landlines or numbers on suppression/DNC lists you're required to respect.
- Respect quiet hours and frequency caps. Don't be misleading. Prefer texting recipients who have a plausible reason to expect contact.
- Laws vary by country/state and change — this playbook is operational guidance, not legal advice. Verify the rules for your market.
