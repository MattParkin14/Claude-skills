# Facebook DM Outreach

First contact with a local business found on Facebook, usually a new
business posting in a community group, or a business whose only presence is
a Facebook page. The goal of the first DM is one thing: **a yes to "want me
to send you the link?"** Building the site is `demo-sites`' job.

Trigger on: "outreach fb dm", "fb dm", "DM this business", screenshots of a
Facebook page or group post with a request to reach out, or a pasted reply
from a prospect who was DM'd.

## Before drafting

Pull from the screenshots: business name as they write it, owner's first
name, which group the post was in, location and service area, services,
anything they made that's worth a genuine compliment (logo, photos).

Quick checks (silent, mention only if relevant):
- Do they have a Google Maps listing? No listing strengthens the pitch, but
  never say it to them as a criticism.
- How old is the post? Under a day is warm, so send now.

Log the lead in the "FB Outreach" Google Doc if the Drive connector is
available, and check it for the user's voice examples.

## The first DM: rules

- **Send fast.** A group post is warm for about a day. Don't hold the DM
  back to build a demo first.
- **Offer the link, don't build first.** Build only on a yes (hand off to
  `demo-sites`), then get the link to them within about a day.
- **Say where you saw them** ("saw your post in the Helensville group").
- **One genuine, specific compliment** on something they made, such as
  their logo. Keep it short.
- **Frame it as an addition**: "so people can find you on Google as well as
  Facebook". **Never tell them what they're doing wrong or what they lack**
  ("you won't come up on Google", "you've only got a Facebook page"). From a
  stranger it reads as criticism.
- **No price in the first DM.** A number gives them a reason to say no
  before they've seen anything.
- **Don't claim a site exists** unless it does. "I've got an idea for what a
  site could look like" is honest.
- **End with one easy yes/no question**, plus "no cost to have a look".
- Short, casual, NZ voice. Three short paragraphs max. No emojis unless the
  user's voice examples use them.

## Templates

Default:

> Hey [Name], saw your post in the [Group] group. Sharp logo, by the way!
>
> I build websites and Google listings for local tradies around [Area], so
> people searching for [their service] can find you on Google as well as
> Facebook.
>
> I've got an idea for what a site for [Business] could look like, using
> your logo. Want me to send you the link? No cost to have a look.

Shorter:

> Hey [Name], saw your post in the [Group] group. Sharp logo, by the way!
>
> I build websites for local tradies around [Area]. Happy to put one
> together for [Business] using your logo so you can see how it'd look.
> Want me to send you a link?

Swap the compliment for whatever is genuinely good about their page. If
nothing is, drop the line rather than inventing one.

Deliver drafts with the message compose widget: the recommended version
first, plus one alternative.

## Handling replies

- **Yes / "sure, send it"** → hand off to `demo-sites` using the Facebook
  page as recon. Send the link with the "rough" framing:
  > "Here it is: [url]. It's rough, just a first pass using what's on your
  > page. Have a scroll and let me know what you'd change."
- **"How much?"** → give the number plainly, without padding, and still
  offer the link so they see what it gets them.
- **"Already sorted" / no** → thank them, wish them well, stop. No second
  pitch.
- **No reply** → at most one light nudge a few days later, then leave it.

After the link is sent, `demo-sites` Step 7 follow-ups apply (the link
message counts as the first touch).

## Check before it's sent

1. No price.
2. No criticism of their current setup.
3. Doesn't claim a site has been built if it hasn't.
4. Compliment is specific and true.
5. Ends with a single yes/no question.
