# email-campaign

A distilled playbook for turning an existing email list into direct revenue by
running a quarterly "conversion event" — modeled on a campaign that produced
$2.4M in 5 days from a 464K-email segment (~$6 per email sent). The core
insight: once a lead is on your list, the cost to acquire them again is $0, so
every email-driven sale drops straight to the bottom line.

## Mindset

- Treat the list as the business's investment account: ads/outbound are the
  paycheck, email is the 401(k) that compounds. Email revenue also raises
  enterprise value twice — it's pure bottom-line profit (businesses trade on
  profit multiples) and it smooths revenue volatility, because a list doesn't
  crash overnight the way ad performance can.
- Offers ≈ money: revenue correlates with how many offers you make, but every
  ask spends goodwill. Manage a **give-to-ask ratio** — deposit value emails
  between promos so you've earned the right to ask. It is fine to re-ask;
  people get busy and abandon carts, they aren't offended by a second ask you
  have earned.

## The play: quarterly cleanup

Run this once per quarter. Between events, send pure-value emails.

### 1. Build the conversion event

- Pick a **challenge or lead magnet pulled from the existing product**: a
  complete solution to a narrow problem whose solving naturally creates the
  next problem your paid offer solves (e.g. weight loss: "lose 7 lbs in 7
  days" → now let's talk long-term).
- Score it against the value equation:
  **(dream outcome × perceived likelihood) ÷ (time delay × effort)**.
  A good challenge maximizes all four: a clearly named outcome people want,
  guided steps so it feels guaranteed, a short time box (7 days), and a
  "we give you every step" framing so it feels easy.
- Give it a deadline and a big promise you know you can deliver. The
  challenge's job is to *approximate a brand relationship fast* — cram six
  months of warm-up into seven days for people who barely know you.

### 2. Test on a sub-segment first

- Send to **≤10–15% of the list** on the first run. If it flops, you haven't
  burned the whole list; when you find the mistakes, you'll be glad the other
  85% never saw them.
- Do 1–3 test runs (they reran the identical play on a fresh segment the very
  next month: $2.2M) before rolling to the full list with the learnings.
- Split-test on every run: subject line, email opener, CTA/link, landing
  page, and the name of the challenge itself. These small tests can double or
  triple campaign throughput.

### 3. Benchmark the funnel

Reference numbers from the source campaign (warm-ish list, many non-buyers,
$2,000 offer, $8,000 upsell):

| Stage | Rate | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Open | 18% | 464K → 83K |
| Click (of openers) | ~5% | 83K → landing page |
| Registered | — | 10K |
| Bought (of registrants) | 8% | 800 × $2,000 = $1.6M |
| Took upsell (of buyers) | 20% | +$800K |
| **Revenue per email sent** | | **~$6** |

Use these to sanity-check a campaign plan and to spot which stage of a
user's funnel is underperforming.

### 4. Rewrap, don't rebuild

- Keep the deliverable the same; change the **wrapper** each quarter — new
  name, subject lines, landing page, seasonal or pain-point hook ("Lean by
  Halloween", "Slim for Summer", "6-Week Booty Blast").
- Small/local lists fatigue fastest, so they need the most wrapper variety.
- Once four quarterly promos are tested, reuse the same annual calendar every
  year — they become autopilot revenue events.

### 5. Vary the medium

Sell the same offer through different mediums (email, webinar, live workshop,
in-person event, phone). People judge goodwill per medium, so this earns extra
"at-bats" without offer fatigue — and each medium supports a different price
point for the same information.

## Judgment calls

- Single-source playbook: these tactics come from one operator's case study
  (Alex Hormozi), not a consensus of many creators. The funnel numbers are one
  business's results at a $2,000 price point on a warm list — treat them as
  order-of-magnitude benchmarks, not promises.
- The source deliberately skips deliverability/domain-reputation mechanics but
  flags that a regular send cadence matters for them; if the user's list has
  been fully cold for years, warm the sending domain and cadence up before a
  big promo.
- Organizational note from the source: the winning idea came from the
  marketing director, not the founder. When advising, favor testing a
  team-sourced campaign idea cheaply (small segment) over debating it.
