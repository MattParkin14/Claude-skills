# Copywriting Playbook

Distilled from 16 expert videos. Actions are ranked by cross-source consensus (how many of the 16 independently taught them). Read this before drafting.

## The top actions (ranked by consensus)

1. **Research the reader before you write — 90% of copy happens before the first word (~13/16).**
   Build a picture of one specific person: their fears, desires, and goals *at each stage* of their journey (a "customer codex," not just a static avatar). Mine their own language from Reddit, Amazon reviews, and Facebook-group/forum comments. You cannot persuade someone you haven't understood.

2. **Lead with the hook / headline / first line; every line's job is to earn the next one (~11/16).**
   The headline is the most important text — it must (a) say what this is / why it matters and (b) make them care, and be understood in ~2 seconds ("one Mississippi, two Mississippi"). The first sentence of a body/email must open a loop — never open with pleasantries. First line → second line (Sugarman): line one exists only to get line two read.

3. **Sell benefits and outcomes, not features (~8/16).**
   People buy the result. Formula: **"[feature] so you can [benefit]."** Push every fact to a promise ("our batteries have copper tops" → "You can't top the copper top"). Tie the surface result to a deeper status/identity/biological need — don't let readers fill the blank themselves.

4. **Build on a proven structure that matches the audience temperature (~9/16).**
   Copy is assembled, not written. Pick a skeleton (see "Structures" below) so you hit the beats — then rewrite it so it doesn't read like a template.

5. **Be specific, concrete, and falsifiable (~7/16).**
   Specificity creates trust; details are believed. "Don't talk, only point" — get off the adjective trail and point to proof ("he reads on the tube," not "he's smart"; the 50-year gold chart, not "great investment"). **Start with a fact** — it guarantees you actually said something, and behind every fact is a story. Turn adjectives into realities ("don't say inequality — show the graph").

6. **Trigger emotion and use core psychology (~8/16).**
   People decide with feelings and justify with logic. Choose the dominant emotion: **toward pleasure** or **away from pain**; agitate pain vividly so it outweighs the price ("twist the knife"), then relieve it. Core triggers (Cashvertising / Cattoni): loss aversion, social proof, authority, reciprocity, commitment/consistency, scarcity.

7. **One big idea; one clear CTA. Cut everything that doesn't serve it (~7/16).**
   The Rule of One (Masterson): the strongest messages center on ONE idea that is easy to understand, easy to believe, and interesting/unique. Avoid "tossed-salad" copy. Every sub-point reinforces the single spear tip. One asset = one job = one CTA (clear request + obvious control). Kill "and": do one thing well.

8. **Prove it — credibility, social proof, risk reversal (~7/16).**
   After any big claim the reader thinks "how do I know this is true?" Answer with testimonials (show, don't just tell — images/screenshots), numbers, case studies, credentials, guarantees. On sales pages: curiosity → **trust** → urgency.

9. **Write simply; it should sound human and easy (~7/16).**
   Short sentences, short paragraphs (aim ≤2 lines — "monkey butts, easy to swing between"), everyday words, line-by-line (one idea per line). **Read it aloud** to find every clunk and boring patch. Aim below ~grade-7 reading level (Hemingway app / Flesch-Kincaid). Kaplan's Law of Words: any word not working for you is working against you.

10. **It's rewriting, not writing — draft fast, then iterate and test (~7/16).**
    Freewrite the first draft (forget grammar), let it cool, then rework. Great ads take many rewrites. Write 4–5 versions of a key line — the freedom to be terrible unlocks the good one, and showing people options gets far better feedback. Split-test headlines/CTAs when you can. Standards are your work: ship only when you can't remove anything.

11. **Differentiate with a named unique mechanism (~5/16).**
    "Never write an ad a competitor could sign." Give your method a curiosity-provoking name ("the 2-Hour Marketing Funnel," "telescope syndrome"). Name the reader's problem better than they can and they'll assume you have the solution. "Copy that can't be copied."

12. **Match message to Awareness × Sophistication (Eugene Schwartz) (~3/16 but foundational).**
    **Awareness** (5 stages): Unaware → Problem-aware → Solution-aware → Product-aware → Most-aware. Cold/problem-aware copy names the problem and the key benefit; solution-aware leads with the unique mechanism + proof; most-aware just needs the offer + urgency.
    **Sophistication:** the more similar claims a market has heard, the further you must escalate — plain claim → bigger claim → **mechanism** → bigger/unique mechanism → identification/positioning.

13. **Study and swipe real copy; model the greats (~6/16).**
    Keep a swipe file of ads/emails/headlines that grabbed you (Facebook Ad Library, Google Ads Transparency Center, high-view YouTube titles, tabloid/Cosmo blurbs, swiped.co; subscribe to great lists with a burner email). Analyze *why* it works, then reverse-engineer the mechanism into a new context. The **531 method:** read 5 similar promos, bullet-outline 3, marry 1 as your structural template. Foundational reading the sources cite: *Breakthrough Advertising* (Schwartz), *Cashvertising* (Whitman), Scientific Advertising (Hopkins), Ogilvy, Halbert's Boron Letters, Caples.

14. **Use AI as a trained assistant, never a ghostwriter (~6/16, AI-focused sources).**
    Garbage in = garbage out; feed it context, not "write me an email." Give it: role + exemplars to emulate, the brand/voice profile + audience codex, a structure, real research and *attributed* stats, and a length. Then **always rewrite** — add story, voice, quirks, and cut the AI-slop tells (blacklist in `brief-and-refine.md`). Save reusable projects/skills so quality compounds. It's a draft (80–90%), not a final.

## Persuasion foundations (apply to every line)

**Harry Dry's 3-rule sentence test** — run every line through it:
1. **Can I visualize it?** (concrete beats abstract — we remember what we can picture). If a word is abstract, zoom in: rewrite it, asking "what do I actually mean?" until you reach a concrete object ("regain fitness" → "off the couch" → "run 5K" → *Couch to 5K*).
2. **Can I falsify it?** (true/false statements make readers sit up; adjectives don't).
3. **Can nobody else say it?** (differentiation; a competitor couldn't sign it).
Three no's = rubbish. Three yes's = you're onto something. Model line: *"New Balance — worn by supermodels in London and dads in Ohio."*

**HubSpot's 4 foundations** — every strong line is: **Specific** (a real product truth) · **Emotional** (feel, don't just state) · **Memorable** (rhyme/rhythm/contrast) · **Positioned** (taps culture or challenges the norm).

**A → B:** the reader's current attitude is the start line, the desired attitude the finish. Your only job is to move them from A to B.

**Conflict / enemy in every piece:** hinge on an implied "but." Three enemy types (ABC): a different **A**pproach, a **B**elief, or a **C**ompetitor. Relative contrast makes things vivid (pickle juice makes orange juice taste sweeter).

**Design and copy are one:** write in the real context (the email tool, the page, the ad mocked up among other ads), not a blank doc — see it as the reader will.

## Structures & templates (pick by asset + temperature)

**Temperature → structure:** cold → AIDA / VSL (earn attention first). warm (on a list) → PAS. hot → offer + proof + urgency.

**AIDA** (ads / cold): Attention (hook) → Interest (build trust/relevance) → Desire (amplify the want, proof) → Action (one CTA + offer).

**PAS** (warm email/social): Problem → Agitate (twist the knife, make it vivid) → Solution (your product relieves it).

**7-step direct-response letter** (Halbert): (1) get attention → (2) give a reason to be interested (expand the central selling idea) → (3) reason to believe → (4) prove it → (5) itemize all benefits → (6) how to order → (7) tell them to order now.

**PROOF** — pass any draft through it: **P**romise (center on the main value) · **R**esults (stats/testimonials/numbers) · **O**bjections (answer them) · **O**utcome (what they get) · **F**riction (risk reversal, urgency, clear CTA — remove friction to the click).

**VSL / video sales letter:** big-promise intro (positive, no fear-mongering) → depersonalize the negative ("men over 40," not "you") → establish authority/proof → tease the named mechanism in visual language → call out limited time/money/energy → discovery story (relatable pain + light-bulb) → future-pacing (let them imagine the result; stays compliant) → offer, reiterated ~3× with scarcity → FAQ handling every objection → CTA framed as *giving them the chance to buy*.

**Sales page / landing page:**
- Homepage skeleton (Kennelly): full-width hero (image/video, headline ≤2 lines & ≤50% width, subhead, CTA) → full-width intro (heading, text, CTA) → alternating image/text feature-benefit blocks (each with its own CTA) → 3-column value props **or** testimonials → full-width final pitch. Images must relate to the text; tell one cohesive story.
- Sales-page headline stack (Tyson 4D): **eyebrow** (calls out the audience) → **main** (who + result + named system + timeframe) → **deck** (handles objections: "even if…"). Then VSL → button → testimonials → body. Add **scroll-stoppers** (mini-headlines) + simple visuals for skimmers. Use a **"Say goodbye to [pains] / Say hello to [dream state]"** table and/or **"This is for you if… / not for you if…"** to qualify. Put the CTA above the fold. Split-test.

**Email — structures that convert (Tyson 4D / Griffin):** the email's job is usually to **sell the click** (curiosity + intent), not the sale — the next page sells. Length ~200–400 words.
- **QDF/QISDF:** Question (common) → Intrigue (tease, don't reveal) → Story (relatable) → Discovery → why/how it works (withhold) → Freebie link (carrying a soft CTA).
- **HIL:** Hypothetical scenario → Intrigue → Lesson + CTA.
- **QBOT:** Qualifying question → Brag (humbly) → Offer → Testimonials → CTA link.
- Subject line's 4 jobs: qualify (repel non-buyers), grab attention / pattern-interrupt, stay relevant, pass Dan Kennedy's **classified test** (a reader grasps it and could act from the line alone).
- Email angles: social proof, break a limiting belief, handle an objection, educate/nurture. A before/after story does several at once.

**Headlines & hooks — drills and formulas:**
- Do two things in one sentence: communicate the core message + make them care (Asana "Work on big ideas without the busy work"; Weight Watchers "Weight loss and wellness designed for you").
- Reframe numbers to your advantage ("You'll spend 22,000 hours writing — spend 2 learning to do it well": expand the timeframe, flip cost→saving, use parallelism).
- Explain the new via the known ("tougher than an F-150, faster than a 911").
- Emotionally charged words (secret, magical, instant, reliable); avoid worn buzzwords (passion, revolutionary, dynamic).
- Practice: write headlines for 5 different customer avatars; rework clickworthy headlines into new angles; write 10 CTA variations; rewrite one opening line 5 ways.
