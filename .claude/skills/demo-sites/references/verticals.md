# Vertical specs

Read the one block that matches. Each block sets the second section, the
primary CTA, the open loop, and when to walk in. Everything else in
SKILL.md stays the same.

Ordered by how well the demo play works: food and tattoo are the easiest
sells, dentists the hardest.

---

## Café / food

- **Pitching:** owner or manager, usually on the floor. Often the person
  who makes your coffee.
- **Second section:** the menu. Six to ten items, not the whole board.
  Lead with what they're known for.
- **Primary CTA:** Call — but Directions does the real work here. People
  looking up a café want to know if it's open and where it is.
- **Open loop:** greyed-out "Order online".
- **Photo:** their food, shot by them, from Instagram. Interior shots
  read as generic; a flat white with their latte art is unmistakably
  theirs.
- **Prices:** the highest-risk vertical for stale prices — cafés move
  them constantly. If the source post is older than three months, run the
  menu without prices and open with "I left the prices off, what are they
  actually now?"
- **Walk in:** 2–4pm. Never 7–9am or 12–1:30pm.
- **Lands because:** they see their own flat white at their own price on
  a phone. That specific moment is the sale.
- **Trap:** listing an item they've dropped. Cross-check the menu against
  their most recent three posts.

## Tattoo studio

- **Pitching:** the artist-owner. Studios are often single-operator or a
  small collective, so you're talking to the decision-maker immediately.
- **Second section:** portfolio grid. Six to nine pieces, their best
  work, uncropped. This section *is* the pitch — their existing Instagram
  is a grid, so show them what a grid looks like when it's theirs.
- **Primary CTA:** Enquire (tel: or a mailto). Not "book" — tattoo
  bookings run through consults.
- **Open loop:** greyed-out "Request a consult".
- **Photo:** their strongest single piece as the hero, not the shopfront.
- **Prices:** never. Tattoo pricing is per-piece and quoting a number
  will actively annoy them. Use "from" day rates only if they publish
  them.
- **Walk in:** early afternoon, mid-week. Avoid Saturdays entirely.
- **Lands because:** artists care about presentation more than any other
  vertical on this list, and Instagram's compression is a live sore
  point.
- **Trap:** using another artist's work from a collective's shared
  account. Check the credit in the caption.

## Trades (builder, sparky, plumber, landscaper)

- **Pitching:** the owner, by phone or at the yard. There's often no
  premises to walk into — this is the one vertical where a call to
  arrange a look at your phone beats a cold walk-in.
- **Second section:** before/after gallery, three pairs. Failing that,
  three finished jobs with a one-line description of what the job was.
- **Primary CTA:** Get a quote (tel:).
- **Open loop:** greyed-out "Request a quote online".
- **Photo:** a finished job, wide. Not a van, not a logo.
- **Prices:** never. Quoting a trade's prices is the fastest way to lose
  them.
- **Walk in:** call between 7–8am or after 4pm; they're on tools during
  the day and will not read a text at 11am.
- **Lands because:** most trades have a Facebook page and nothing else,
  and they know it costs them jobs.
- **Trap:** they may be flat-out and not want more work. Ask what their
  book looks like before you pitch — if they're booked to Christmas,
  reposition the site as "so you can stop answering the tyre-kickers".

## Barber / salon

- **Pitching:** owner, usually cutting. Wait for them to finish a head.
- **Second section:** services with prices. Barbers publish these on a
  wall and change them rarely, so this is the safest priced vertical.
- **Primary CTA:** Book (tel:).
- **Open loop:** greyed-out "Book online" — the strongest open loop on
  this list, because online booking is the thing they actually want.
- **Photo:** the chairs, or a cut. Their interior is usually good and
  they've usually paid for it.
- **Prices:** safe to include if visible on the wall in any recent photo.
- **Walk in:** Tuesday to Thursday, 10–11:30am or 2–3:30pm.
- **Lands because:** phone bookings interrupt their work. The demo hints
  at fewer interruptions.
- **Trap:** if they already use Fresha, Booksy or Squire, the online
  booking open loop is dead — they have it. Pivot the loop to a gallery
  or gift vouchers.

## Services (gym, studio, cleaner, mobile groomer, tutor)

- **Pitching:** owner. Varies wildly — check whether there's a premises
  at all before planning a walk-in.
- **Second section:** the offer block. What they do, for whom, at what
  commitment level — three to five options with prices if published.
- **Primary CTA:** Call, or Book a trial if they run one.
- **Open loop:** greyed-out "Book a free trial" or "Sign up online".
- **Photo:** people using the space, mid-activity. Empty rooms sell
  nothing.
- **Prices:** include if published anywhere public — for these,
  membership pricing is usually stable and already advertised.
- **Walk in:** off-peak for their trade. Gyms: 10am–3pm, never 5–7pm.
- **Lands because:** they're usually competing with a chain that has a
  proper site.
- **Trap:** the category is broad. If you can't state their single
  offer in one sentence, you haven't done enough recon to build.

## Dentist / clinic

Hardest of the six. Treat differently or skip.

- **Pitching:** you will meet a receptionist, not the owner. The
  practice manager is the real gatekeeper and the dentist is chairside
  all day. Expect two visits minimum.
- **Second section:** services with indicative pricing *only if they
  publish it* — many don't, deliberately.
- **Primary CTA:** Book an appointment (tel:).
- **Open loop:** greyed-out "Book online".
- **Photo:** the practice interior or the team. Never stock teeth. Never
  a procedure.
- **Prices:** only from their own published fee list. Never infer.
- **Walk in:** mid-morning, mid-week. Ask for the practice manager by
  role, and ask when they're free rather than pitching on the spot.
- **Lands because:** clinic sites are usually old and not mobile-first,
  and they're spending real money on Google Ads pointed at them.
- **Trap:** compliance. Do not write anything that reads as a health
  claim, a treatment outcome, or a guarantee. Keep copy to services,
  hours, location, and booking. If in doubt, leave the copy blank and
  let them supply it — that's a conversation, not a gap.
